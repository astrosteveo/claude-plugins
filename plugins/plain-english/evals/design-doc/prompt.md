Void Sector is command-first: you give orders and the ship's computer flies them, and you take the controls when it counts. The holomap is where you read the fight and give those orders, and it zooms out into the sector and region maps.

## The idea: captain, not pilot

The ship is advanced enough to be commanded: you set the intent, the computer does the maneuvering, and you grab the controls to outfly it. Four rules keep that honest.

- **Command first, fly when it counts.** Orders are the default way to play. The computer is competent, not optimal: it holds a facing's center (one battery bears) and reacts a beat late. Hand flying stays the better play at the margins, such as overlapping two weapons' arcs.
- **Your input takes over at once.** Any steering or throttle ends a running order, and Resume hands it back. Strafe and vertical thrust are the exception: they add to what the order flies. All stop ends the order and stops the ship. (The prototype first blended steering into the order for about a second. The game ends the order at once.)
- **The holomap is the bridge.** You read the fight and give orders on one hologram in the middle of the command center, and zoom out of it into the sector and region maps. Hovering a contact lights it everywhere at once. The holomap is never part of the world.
- **Components gate reach, detail and automation, never the basics.** Every ship starts with a readable holomap and the core orders.

It also suits an MMO. An order is plain data on the ship, with a queue of waypoints for a course, so it is one small message the server carries out. That is how EVE copes with a one-second server tick. Pirate captains and NPCs give orders the same way.

## How you command

The mouse works like WoW's, with right-click as the order button. The mouse looks around and commands; WASD is the stick. Every key can be rebound. Stance, Resume and All stop each have an action and a key of their own.
